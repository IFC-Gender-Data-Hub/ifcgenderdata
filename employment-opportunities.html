Beyond Employment Numbers: Understanding Women's Experiences at Work

Women's participation in employment tells only part of the story. Where women work, the occupations they hold, and their experiences entering and remaining in employment offer a broader perspective on economic opportunities and workplace inclusion.

IFC Gender Data Stories | 2025

Women's representation at entry level does not always match their share of the overall workforce

Women's share of the profiled workforce has increased over time, although representation varies across regions. Comparing women's share of all employees with their share of entry-level employees provides another perspective on how women are represented at the starting level of firms. The relationship between these two measures differs across regions.

Women remain concentrated in certain industries, with lower representation in others

The industries in which women work also vary considerably. Women account for larger shares of employment in some service sectors, while their representation is lower in several industrial sectors. Comparing Revelio's profiled firms with Equileap's publicly listed companies provides two perspectives on women's representation across industries.

Source: Revelio Labs and Equileap via World Bank Group.

Women's occupational profiles differ across firm sizes

Beyond the industries in which women work, the occupations they hold provide another perspective on their employment experiences. The distribution of women across occupational groups varies by firm size, offering a closer look at the types of roles represented in different firms.

Women's hiring rates relative to men's vary across regions and over time

Hiring rates offer another perspective on how women and men enter firms. Across most regions and for much of the period, women's hiring rates are higher than men's when measured relative to their respective employment bases. The size of this difference varies across regions and over time.

Chart 5. Hiring rates for women and men by region, 2015–2025.

Source: Revelio Labs via World Bank Group. Hiring rate is hires as a share of employment.

Sectors with higher hiring rates do not necessarily retain women for longer

Beyond who enters firms, the length of time employees remain with their employers offers another perspective on employment patterns. Retention measures the share of women who remain with the same employer after twelve months. Comparing hiring and retention rates across sectors reveals differences in both workforce entry and employment continuity. Research on employee turnover and job tenure also highlights the importance of looking beyond recruitment to understand employment continuity (Hom, Roberson, and Ellis 2008; Hollister and Smith 2014).

Chart 6. Hiring and retention rates of women by sector, 2025.

Source: World Bank Group. Retention rate is the share of women who remain with the same employer after twelve months.

Equal-opportunity hiring and training policies are widespread, but employee resource groups remain uncommon

Company policies offer another perspective on workplace inclusion. Formal commitments to equal-opportunity hiring and access to training and career development are important elements of organizational practice. Dedicated employee resource groups for women provide a different form of workplace support.

CONCLUSION

Creating workplaces where women can thrive

Expanding women's economic opportunities requires more than increasing their participation in employment. It also means creating workplaces where women have opportunities to develop their skills, advance professionally, and sustain meaningful employment over time. The way organizations recruit, develop, and retain employees, as well as the policies they put in place to support gender inclusion, shapes the broader landscape of opportunities available to women. Moving from commitments to meaningful workplace inclusion requires attention not only to who gets hired, but also to the conditions, opportunities, and support that enable women to thrive throughout their working lives.
